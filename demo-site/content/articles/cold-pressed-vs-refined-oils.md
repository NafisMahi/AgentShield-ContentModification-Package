---
title: "Cold-Pressed vs. Refined Oils: What Actually Changes"
author: "Marco Diaz"
date: "May 21, 2026"
excerpt: "The processing method changes flavour, nutrients, and how you should cook with it."
---
Refined oils are produced with high heat and often chemical solvents, then bleached and deodorised. The result is a neutral, shelf-stable oil — but the process strips natural flavour and destroys much of the Vitamin E and polyphenols the original fruit or seed contained.

Cold-pressed oils use low-temperature mechanical extraction. Because nothing is overheated, they keep their aroma, colour, and a good share of their micronutrients. The trade-off is a lower smoke point, so they shine in dressings and finishing rather than deep frying.

A simple rule: cook with refined oils when you need high heat and no flavour; reach for cold-pressed when taste and nutrition matter more than the temperature.
